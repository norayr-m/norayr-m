THE LIVER LOBULE IS HEXAGONAL

A liver lobule IS a hexagon. Not a metaphor. Portal triads at corners → blood enters Central vein at center → blood exits 5,000 hepatocytes per lobule ~500,000 lobules per liver Zone 1 (periportal): high O₂, first-pass metabolism Zone 3 (pericentral): low O₂, CYP450 expression, DILI target
This is not a metaphor. A liver lobule is literally hexagonal. Portal triads at the six corners bring blood in. The central vein at the center drains it out. Five thousand hepatocytes per lobule. Oxygen decreases radially from the portal to the central vein, creating three metabolic zones. Zone three, near the centre, is where most drug metabolism happens — and where drug-induced liver injury strikes first. Our hex grid engine was built for this geometry.

THE METABOLIC KERNEL

Per hepatocyte, per tick: SENSE: Read O₂ concentration (scent field) Read drug concentration (scent field) Read neighbour metabolite levels COMPUTE: CYP450 metabolism: Michaelis-Menten kinetics V = Vmax × [S] / (Km + [S]) Produce metabolites, consume O₂ Update damage accumulation EMIT: Write metabolite output (scent emission) Update ternary state (+1 → 0 → -1) If damage > threshold → necrosis (entity = EMPTY)
The compute kernel replaces predator-prey logic with Michaelis-Menten enzyme kinetics. Each hepatocyte reads oxygen and drug concentration from the scent fields. It computes CYP 450 metabolism — the standard equation for enzyme activity. It produces metabolites, consumes oxygen, and accumulates damage. If damage exceeds a threshold, the cell dies. Necrosis. Same sense, compute, emit pattern. Different formula inside.

DRUG CLEARANCE SIMULATION

Timeline of a drug dose: t=0 Drug injected at portal vein → set scent field at boundary nodes t=1-10 Drug diffuses through lobule → scent propagation (same as savanna) t=10+ Hepatocytes metabolise drug → CYP450 kernel runs per cell → metabolites produced (secondary scent) t=50+ Zone 3 cells accumulate damage → ternary: +1 → 0 → -1 (DILI) t=100+ Necrosis spreads from Zone 3 inward → same pattern as trampling in savanna t=500+ Surviving cells regenerate → same pattern as grass regrowth
A drug dose simulation. At time zero, the drug enters through the portal vein — we set the scent field at the boundary. The drug diffuses through the lobule, exactly like scent in the savanna. Hepatocytes metabolise the drug using CYP 450 enzymes. Zone three cells, which see the drug last but metabolise most aggressively, accumulate damage first. Necrosis spreads inward — the same spatial pattern as trampling. And afterwards, surviving cells regenerate — the same pattern as grass regrowth. The biology is different. The computation is identical.

WHY THIS MATTERS

Drug-Induced Liver Injury (DILI): • #1 cause of drug withdrawal from market • #1 cause of acute liver failure in the US • ~$2 billion per failed drug candidate • Current testing: animal models (poor human prediction) What a digital twin enables: • Test drug on virtual human liver BEFORE clinical trials • Patient-specific: vary CYP450 genotype per simulation • Dose-response: sweep drug concentrations automatically • Combination drugs: test interactions computationally • Overnight on a laptop, not months in a lab
Drug-induced liver injury is the number one cause of drug withdrawal from the market. Number one cause of acute liver failure in the United States. Each failed drug candidate costs roughly two billion dollars. Current testing relies on animal models that poorly predict human outcomes. A digital twin changes this. Test the drug on a virtual human liver before clinical trials. Vary the patient's genetics — different CYP 450 genotypes process drugs differently. Sweep drug concentrations automatically. Test drug combinations computationally. Overnight on a laptop. Not months in a lab.
